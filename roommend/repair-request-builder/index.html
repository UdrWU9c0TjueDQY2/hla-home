Location Kitchen, cabinet beside the sink.

Observation The lower cupboard door rubs against the drawer when opened. There is a scrape along its top edge.

When and effect First noticed this week. The door now needs repositioning by hand before it will close.

Requested next step Please review the issue and suggest the next step.

A BETTER FIRST MESSAGE
Observation beats assumption.
A vague note
“The kitchen cupboard is broken. The hinges must be faulty.”
The location is broad and the cause is a guess.
A more useful note
“The lower cupboard beside the sink rubs the next drawer when opened. I first noticed it this week. Could you review it and let me know the next step?”
The recipient can identify the item and understand the request.
A repair request template you can use anywhere
Location → item → observation → timing → effect on use → requested next step. Optional details can be left out when you do not know them. A photo reference can help distinguish two similar items, but this tool does not accept or inspect photos.
For a routine request, keep the brief about one issue. If you have several unrelated jobs, make separate briefs so replies and next steps stay attached to the right item.




Record what happened at the repair visit.


Keep the original request beside the visitor’s account of the work. Add what you can observe during ordinary use, who will take the next step, and when they will check back.

Use the handoff without printing

  1. Start with the room, item, visit date, and original observation. A room label is enough for a shared copy.
  2. Record what the visitor said they did or deferred. Separately note what you observed afterward, or write “not observed yet.”
  3. Choose “settled for now,” “follow-up needed,” or “waiting for a reply.” Give the next action to one person and choose a check-back date.


Example: The kitchen cupboard rubbed against the drawer. The visitor reported adjusting a hinge. The door still catches during normal use. Status: follow-up needed. The household coordinator will share the observation on Friday.

Before you use the brief.
Is this a formal notice to a landlord?
No. It is a general communication template, not a legal notice or a statement about repair responsibilities or deadlines.
Does this tell me whether a problem is urgent?
No. The builder cannot assess a situation. Do not use it as an emergency reporting service or wait for it to decide whether you need immediate help.
Where does the information go?
The builder runs in your browser and does not submit or save these fields. Copying places the brief on your clipboard. Downloading saves a plain-text copy on your device; the file remains there after you clear the form. Review it before sharing through another app.
Can I use it for a follow-up?
Yes. Select “Update on a previously reported issue” and include the earlier report date in the timing field if you know it. The tool does not check whether anyone received an earlier message.
