What does the 7-day family emergency plan challenge cover?

Seven tasks, one a day: find your shutoffs, set contacts and meeting places, make an Emergency Card, gather documents, build a 72-hour kit from Ready.gov's list, sort out pets, dependants and a neighbour, then walk the plan and set a review date. Each task has a "done when" line so you know when to stop.

Download the printable 7-day tracker (PDF, one page)

  1. Find the shutoffs

    Walk the house with your phone's flashlight: water main, gas valve, electrical panel. Photograph each and note the tool you need (many gas valves need a wrench). New-home day one, because nobody else knows where they are either.

    Done when: Water main, gas valve, breaker panel located and photographed

  2. Contacts and two meeting places

    Pick an out-of-area contact everyone texts if local lines are jammed, one meeting place outside the home (the mailbox, a neighbour's drive) and one outside the neighbourhood (a school, a library). Write the addresses, not just the names.

    Done when: Out-of-area contact + two meeting places written down

  3. Emergency Card

    Who to call first, allergies, current medications, important conditions. In Family Ready the card is free, saved on the iPhone, and opens in Emergency Mode without internet once the phone is unlocked. Make one per person, with permission.

    Done when: An Emergency Card for each person in the house

  4. Documents in one place

    Insurance policy numbers, ID copies, deeds or lease, vet records, a recent photo of each person and pet. Paper copies in a sealed bag; digital copies in an encrypted backup you can restore elsewhere.

    Done when: Document list made; copies stored in two places

  5. The 72-hour kit

    Ready.gov's baseline: one gallon of water per person per day for several days, a several-day supply of non-perishable food, flashlight, first-aid kit, extra batteries, a whistle, medications, phone chargers. Tick what you own; buy only the gaps.

    Done when: Kit checklist ticked; gaps on a shopping list

  6. Pets, dependants, neighbours

    Carrier, leash and food for each pet; care instructions for anyone who depends on you; one neighbour who has a key and your number. Knock on the door today. It is the whole reason you moved to a street.

    Done when: Pet and dependant notes done; one neighbour knows you

  7. Walk it, then set the review

    Do a ten-minute drill: everyone finds the shutoffs and names the meeting place. Then pick a review schedule, 3, 6 or 12 months, and put the date on the fridge and in the app.

    Done when: Drill done; review date set

Who should do this differently

Households in wildfire or hurricane zones

Do Day 5 first and size the kit for evacuation, not shelter-in-place: go-bags, fuel, and the route. Your county emergency office publishes zone maps; use those over anything generic here.

If you care for someone with complex medical needs

The Emergency Card holds medications and conditions, but a clinician-written care summary belongs in the documents pile too. The app organizes what you enter; it does not give medical advice.

If you want a shared family vault

Trusted-person invitations, household sync and guided reviews sit in Family Ready Plus, a subscription. The free tier is a single-iPhone plan with printable snapshots, which is enough for this challenge.

A made-up week, in a real kind of house

Boxes still in the hall, the previous owner's note on the fridge: "water shutoff is behind the furnace, gas is outside by the meter, you'll need the wrench." Day 1 took eleven minutes and two photographs. Day 2 was an argument about whether the meeting place could be the coffee shop (it can, if it is open at 3 am; it isn't, so it is the library). Day 3 the kids made their own cards and put "I don't know" for medications, which is correct. By Day 7 the drill ran in nine minutes and the review date went on the fridge next to the old note.

Questions before day one

How long does the 7-day family emergency plan challenge take?

About 20 minutes a day for seven days. Day 5, the kit, can take longer if you shop for gaps; Day 1 is the fastest in a house you already know and the slowest in a house you just moved into.

Do I need the app to do the challenge?

No. The printable tracker and this page are enough. The app adds the part paper cannot do: an Emergency Card that opens offline in a high-contrast Emergency Mode, a plan you can edit over time, a kit checklist based on Ready.gov and Get Prepared (Canada), and an encrypted backup you can restore on another device.

What goes on an Emergency Card?

Who to call first, allergies, current medications and important health information. Family Ready's card is free, stored on the iPhone, and never needs a subscription. You can make one for a child, a parent or someone you care for, with their permission.

How much water should a family store?

Ready.gov's kit guidance is one gallon per person per day for several days, for drinking and sanitation. A household of four storing three days needs twelve gallons; the Day 5 task is to count what you have and buy only the gap.

Is Family Ready free?

The free tier includes the Emergency Card, Emergency Mode, offline access, the household plan, the kit checklist, encrypted backup and restore, and an Emergency Snapshot PDF. A Plus subscription adds trusted-person invitations, household sync, guided reviews, date reminders and extra printable packs. The listing says your information stays available if a subscription ends.

Does the app call emergency services for me?

No. Emergency Mode shows your contacts and a prominent reminder to contact local emergency services for anything life-threatening; you tap to call. The app never calls or messages anyone automatically.
