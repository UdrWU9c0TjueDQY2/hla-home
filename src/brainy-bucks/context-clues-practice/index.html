01

Word in focus: sheltered

A quieter corner

The wind pushed the little plant pots across the bench. Mina moved them behind the tall garden wall. In this sheltered spot, the leaves stopped shaking and the pots stayed upright.

What does sheltered mean here?

Open the reasoning

The wall keeps the wind off the pots. The leaves stop shaking and the pots stay upright, so protected from the wind fits this use of sheltered. Nothing in the passage says the new spot is higher or full of flowers.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

02

Word in focus: scarce

The last blue beads

The craft box held a big pile of red beads and only two blue ones. Blue beads were scarce, so the group chose a pattern that needed just one. They saved the other blue bead for a second bracelet.

What does scarce mean here?

Open the reasoning

There are very few blue beads available. The group changes its plan to use fewer of them. That supports the meaning hard to find in enough quantity, rather than a description of their weight or shape.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

03

Word in focus: reluctant

One line at a time

Ari knew the opening line of the play, but stayed beside the curtain when rehearsal began. Ari was reluctant to step onto the stage. After a friend offered to practise the line together, Ari took a small step forward.

What does reluctant mean here?

Open the reasoning

Staying beside the curtain and taking only a small step after encouragement suggest hesitation. The passage does not say Ari is running or cannot hear. Reluctant describes being hesitant or unwilling at first.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

04

Word in focus: sturdy

The model bridge

The first cardboard bridge bent when a small toy bus rolled onto it. The team added folded supports underneath. Their new bridge was sturdy: it held the bus without bending or wobbling.

What does sturdy mean here?

Open the reasoning

Holding the bus without bending or wobbling shows that the bridge is strong and steady. The supports change its strength. The paragraph gives no information about paint, and easy folding would not explain why it holds the bus.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

05

Word in focus: improvise

A rhythm without a drum

The class had planned a rhythm game, but the drum was missing. They decided to improvise. One person tapped a cardboard box while another shook a jar of dry beans, and the group made a new rhythm together.

What does improvise mean here?

Open the reasoning

The box and bean jar replace the planned drum. The group finds a workable way to continue with the materials at hand. That is improvising; they are neither following the exact original plan nor stopping in silence.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

06

Word in focus: inspect

The puzzle edge

One piece would not fit into the puzzle, even though its colours looked right. Jo began to inspect it, turning it slowly and looking closely at each edge. A tiny bend in one corner explained the problem.

What does inspect mean here?

Open the reasoning

Turning the piece and looking closely at every edge are ways to examine it carefully. Inspect means to look at something closely to check it. Hiding or repeatedly counting the piece would not explain the discovery of the bent corner.

Talk together: Which words in the passage support that meaning?

Make room for
the reasoning.

  1. Read the complete sentence. Stop after the passage rather than asking for an instant answer as soon as the highlighted word appears.
  2. Find a useful detail. Ask, "Which words helped you?" The aim is to connect a possible meaning to something in the passage.
  3. Try the meaning in place. Put the chosen meaning into the sentence. Does it still describe what happened? A choice can sound familiar without fitting this particular passage.
  4. Check what remains uncertain. Some texts do not give enough context. It is fine to use a dictionary or ask someone, rather than force a guess.

For a simpler round, discuss two possible meanings instead of three. For an extra challenge, cover the choices and suggest a meaning before comparing it with the explanation. Neither version is a reading-level assessment.

A helpful response to a different answer is, "Show me the clue you used." Then compare it with another detail. These six activities are for practice and conversation; they do not establish mastery or predict school results.

1. bark

A: A dog sound. B: The outside of a tree.

  1. The dog gave one loud bark when the doorbell rang.
  2. Mina rubbed the rough bark on the old tree trunk.
Grown-up key for bark

Sentence 1: A. The dog and the sound point to A. Sentence 2: B. The tree trunk and its rough surface point to B.

2. bat

A: A flying animal. B: A piece of sports equipment.

  1. Jo held the bat firmly and waited for the next pitch.
  2. At dusk, a small bat flew out of the cave.
Grown-up key for bat

Sentence 1: B. Holding it before a pitch points to B. Sentence 2: A. Flying out of a cave points to A.

3. light

A: Not heavy. B: Something that helps us see.

  1. The empty cardboard box was light enough to lift with one hand.
  2. The hallway was dark, so Ari switched on the light.
Grown-up key for light

Sentence 1: A. Being easy to lift points to A. Sentence 2: B. Darkness and switching it on point to B.

4. park

A: A public outdoor place. B: To leave a vehicle in a space.

  1. Dad will park the car beside the shop before we walk inside.
  2. The children met at the park to use the swings.
Grown-up key for park

Sentence 1: B. The car and where to leave it point to B. Sentence 2: A. Meeting outdoors by the swings points to A.

5. ring

A: A sound made by a bell. B: A circular piece of jewellery.

  1. The ring had a tiny blue stone and fit on one finger.
  2. The ring of the bicycle bell made us look up.
Grown-up key for ring

Sentence 1: B. The stone and finger point to B. Sentence 2: A. The bicycle bell and looking up point to A.
