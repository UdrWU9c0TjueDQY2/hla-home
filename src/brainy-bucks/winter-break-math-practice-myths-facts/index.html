Seven myths. Tap each for the fact.

  • Fact

    The well-studied loss is over summer, and even there it is “common, but not inevitable” (Kuhfeld, 2019, from 3.4 million test records). Two weeks in December has far less evidence behind it. Keep a light routine; do not panic.

    Kuhfeld, Kappan, 2019
  • Fact

    The two techniques rated highest for durable learning in the 2013 Dunlosky review were practice testing and distributed practice: short, spread-out retrieval. A packet done in one sitting is the opposite shape.

    Dunlosky et al., 2013
  • Fact

    Rereading, highlighting and summarising were rated low utility in the same review. Answering questions and getting a second try does more than reading the page again.

    Dunlosky et al., 2013
  • Fact

    What helps is the second attempt with a clue, and the question coming back days later. Brainy Bucks is built that way per its listing: no lives lost, no red X, a clue and a second try, missed questions return in later quests.

    App Store listing, 4 Nov 2026
  • Fact

    Distributed practice, the high-utility technique, means spreading the same time out. Ten minutes on 16 days is 160 minutes and far better spaced than two 80-minute Sundays.

    Dunlosky et al., 2013

Build the 10-minute plan for your dates

Pick the grade and the number of days off. The plan alternates new practice, review and a screen-free day, and names the kind of skill for that grade using the subjects Brainy Bucks lists. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    Days marked “screen-free” are the kind of parent-and-child activity the app also lists (materials, steps, easier and harder versions); a deck of cards or a tape measure works too.

    Questions parents ask

    How much math practice should a child do over winter break?

    There is no official number. The research on learning techniques favours short, spaced sessions over long ones, so ten minutes most days is a reasonable target for Kindergarten to Grade 4; older children can do fifteen. The planner above builds that shape for your dates.

    What should they practise?

    Whatever was shaky in December. In Brainy Bucks the daily quest mixes new skills, review and earlier mistakes, and the parent dashboard labels skills Introduced, Practicing, Consistent, Retained or Needs review, from the app’s own answers only.

    Is Brainy Bucks free?

    Per the listing: one adaptive Today’s Quest every day with no time limit, three extra quests a day, hints, second tries, spaced review, the weekly summary and learning plan, a Brain Buddy, one real-life reward and a starter activity set. Plus adds unlimited quests, the full library, reports and up to four child profiles, as a monthly or yearly subscription or a one-time Lifetime.

    Is it safe for a six-year-old to use alone?

    The listing says no accounts, no ads, no chat, no third-party analytics, learning data stays on the device, and Child Mode shows no prices or purchase buttons; purchases and settings sit behind a parent PIN. Rated 4+. We would still sit beside a six-year-old for the first quest.

    Does it cover reading too?

    Yes: phonics, rhyming, sight words, spelling patterns, vocabulary, main idea, sequencing, inference, context clues and short original passages, with on-device read-aloud. Content spans Pre-K to Grade 8 with the deepest coverage from Kindergarten to Grade 4.
