Build the story sequence

A storm in a paper bag

Read a short play with a partner. Choose Pip or Kit. A child and an adult can take a part each, or two children can read together. Try the sound effects with your voice. A paper bag is optional.

Print the one-page script (PDF)

Read the script on screen
Pip
Our play starts with a storm. Ready?
Kit
Ready! I can be the thunder. BOOM!
Pip
That was good thunder. But where is the rain?
Kit
Rain? All we have is this paper bag.
Pip
Try giving it a shake.
Kit
CRACKLE! That sounds like someone opening a snack.
Pip
Try rubbing just one corner. Slowly.
Kit
Shh, shh, shh. Rain.
Pip
Now rub two corners.
Kit
Shh-shh-shh! Now it’s pouring!
Pip
The storm starts small. Then it grows. What comes last?
Kit
One last drop.
Pip
Tap.
Kit
And then?
Pip
Quiet. The storm is over.

Swap parts and read again

Kit says “Rain?” and later “Rain.” Try both aloud. Which one sounds like a question? What did Pip change between the two lines?

After the play, name the sound they tried first, the change that worked, and the final sound. Check your sequence by finding the lines in the script.

Change one sound after the paper-bag play

Pick two words from the play: tap and bag. Each starts a different sound chain for a reader and a clue giver.

Print the one-page word-chain board (PDF)

Use both word chains on screen

An adult reads each clue. The reader says the new word, then writes it in three boxes. Keep the other two sounds. Try SUN. Change its first sound to the first sound in FAN. Blend the new word: FUN.

Start with tap

  1. Change the first sound to the first in moon. It shows roads.
  2. Change the last sound to the last in hat. It lies by a door.
  3. Change the first sound to the first in soup. What I did on a chair yesterday.
  4. Change the middle sound to the middle in fish. What I can do on a chair now.
  5. Change the last sound to the last in cup. Take a little drink.

Start with bag

  1. Change the last sound to the last in hat. It can hang upside down.
  2. Change the first sound to the first in cap. It purrs.
  3. Change the middle sound to the middle in sun. What scissors do.
  4. Change the last sound to the last in lip. You can drink from it.
  5. Change the first sound to the first in pie. A young dog.
Check the two answer chains

tap → map → mat → sat → sit → sip.

bag → bat → cat → cut → cup → pup.

If a word feels tricky, say the old word slowly, swap one sound, then blend again.

READING DETECTIVE · THREE ORIGINAL MINIATURES

What happened before it? What made it happen?

Two things can happen in order without one causing the other. Read a story together, then choose the detail that explains the ending. Before checking, try saying “It happened because …” aloud.

1. The runaway marble

Mira put a sticker on her notebook. She tilted the flat tray, and the marble rolled toward its lower edge. What made the marble roll?

2. The faded arrow

Jules drew a chalk arrow on the path. A bell rang. Rain washed the chalk away. Why was the arrow gone?

3. The quiet bell

Lena folded a map. Then the bicycle bell stopped ringing. She put the map in her pocket. Can the story tell us why the bell stopped?

Try it with a new story: Change one detail so the map really does stop the ringing. Which words now show the cause?

MAKE THE RESULT USEFUL

Ask for a clue, not just an answer.

After an answer, ask: “Which words helped you?” Look for actions that must happen before another action: finding a key before using it, or packing before leaving.

  • Easier: read each event aloud and act it out.
  • Different: cover the cards and retell the story in your own words.
  • Extend: invent one extra event and decide where it belongs.

This is informal practice, not a reading assessment.
