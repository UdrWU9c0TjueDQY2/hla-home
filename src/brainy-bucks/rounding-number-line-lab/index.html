USE IT IN REAL LIFE

Teach the reason before the shortcut.

  1. Find the neighbouring multiples. For 47 to the nearest ten, use 40 and 50. For 347 to the nearest hundred, use 300 and 400.
  2. Compare the two distances. 347 is 47 away from 300 and 53 away from 400, so it rounds to 300.
  3. Handle the halfway case explicitly. 350 is equally far from 300 and 400. This practice uses the common school convention: halfway rounds to the higher multiple.
  4. Keep exact multiples unchanged. 400 rounded to the nearest hundred is still 400. Rounding does not always change a number.

Try it away from the screen

Draw a line from 60 to 70 on scrap paper. Ask your child to place 62, 65 and 68, then explain each decision using “closer” or “halfway.” Repeat with 600 to 700. A correct answer without an explanation is a chance to ask one more gentle question.

What this lab does not measure

These six cards are practice, not a grade-level assessment. They cover non-negative whole numbers only. Other rounding conventions exist in finance and computing; this activity uses halfway-up rounding.
